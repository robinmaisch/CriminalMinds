# Criminal Minds — Replication Study

**A multi-institution replication study on how students plagiarize programming assignments and attempt to evade plagiarism detection.**

We are inviting universities and instructors to participate in a replication of:

> Maisch et al., *Criminal Minds: How First-Year CS Students Plagiarize Code*, FSE Companion 2026.
> DOI: 10.1145/3803437.3805789

The original study was conducted at KIT and KTH and investigated how first-year computer science students modify existing programming solutions while attempting to produce a functionally correct submission that is difficult to recognize as derived from the original.

The study combines:

* participant change logs,
* submitted programs,
* plagiarism-detection results,
* participant survey responses, and
* contextual information about participants and their courses.

The goal of the replication is to investigate whether the findings generalize across institutions, courses, student populations, and potentially programming environments.
It also serves as an engaging challenge for students, confronting them with a novel task.

## What would participation involve?

A participating institution would:

1. recruit students who meet the study's participant criteria;
2. organize a live study session using the provided materials;
3. provide participants with an original programming solution;
4. have participants modify the solution according to the study instructions;
5. collect the resulting code, modification logs, and survey responses;
6. provide the agreed-upon contextual information about the participants and course; and
7. transfer the anonymized study data to the central research team.

We provide the study protocol, participant materials, data collection templates, and recommendations for local adaptation.

## What do we need from a participating institution?

At this stage, we mainly need:

* a suitable introductory programming course or student population;
* a fair number of participating students;
* a programming task and study setting compatible with the protocol;
* a researcher/instructor who can coordinate the local study;
* local ethics/data-protection approval where required; and
* the ability to collect and anonymize the agreed study data.

You do not need to reproduce the original study infrastructure exactly. We will work with you to determine which parts should remain fixed and which can be adapted locally.

## What is fixed and what can be adapted?

The replication distinguishes between:

**Must remain comparable**

* study objective and experimental manipulation;
* participant activity;
* core data collected;
* change-log concept;
* outcome measures;
* key survey constructs.

**Can be adapted**

* programming language;
* course-specific logistics;
* recruitment process;
* session scheduling;
* local ethics procedures;
* technical infrastructure, where necessary.

See [`STUDY.md`](STUDY.md) and [`docs/local-adaptation.md`](docs/local-adaptation.md).
