# Study design

This file describes the study's intent and structure.

## Research goal

We investigate which strategies students use when transforming an existing programming solution into a functionally correct solution that is less recognizable as copied code, and how effective these strategies are against plagiarism detection.

Research Questions:
RQ1: How effectively did the participants plagiarize?
RQ2: Which methods, tools, and sources do the participants use?
RQ3: How do the resulting plagiarisms compare to the original submissions?
RQ4: How does the success of the plagiarism approach relate to the participant's subjective perception of the result?

## What happens in the study?

- Participating students come to the study session.
- They sign a consent form.
- They get a copy of the study task description for their study group.
- They get access to the assignment description and one original code solution for the assignment.
- They get familiar with the assignment task if needed.
- They change the code to make it less recognizable as a copy of the original.
- At any time, they may run functional tests on the current version of the submission (by submitting it to an online submission platform).
- They submit the final solution. They may do this after a set amount of working time or when they are satisfied with their submission.
- They fill out a survey of a few questions regarding their solution and their strategies.
- Before participants start to pack their belongings, you verify that all material is submitted properly. If everything is in order, the participant may leave.

## Participants

We focus mainly on first-term students, many of whom are in the first months of learning programming. Depending on the study program, second-term students may also contribute valuable data.
For advanced students, at least at KIT, we rely on them recognizing the value of exercise for their development as computer scientists and do not usually apply plagiarism detection.

## Experimental conditions

In our setting, we provided an appropriately sized lecture hall (KIT) or computer lab (KTH) for participants to get to work. 
At KIT, we allowed participants to work in groups; however, group work proved far less efficient in the results.
Depending on your setting, state clearly that participants need to bring their own notebooks and power cords; you may also provide extension cords if available and necessary.
On the machine, they need access to the personal materials (e.g., by clicking a link provided via e-mail) and an environment where they can work in the programming language of your course (e.g., IntelliJ + JDK for Java) and Git.

## Data collected

You collect
- the consent form
- the final state of the personal repository
- the change log
- the survey answers
- data about the cohort and, if possible, participants' performance in the course

## What is replicated?

The study's general structure remains the same, including the targeted participant group, study task, artifacts (input and output), and evaluation.

## What may be adapted?

- the assignment task: you may use an assignment task that we provide (in Java), or use one from a previous run of your course. In that case, you need to acquire solutions that you have consent to share with participants and publish as part of the resulting data. We use solutions from TAs that participated in the previous run of the course.
- the time and location of the study session, as feasible.
- the programming language of the course.

# What does a participating institution need to do?

- organize a room for the study
- advertise the study to potential participants
- manage registration for the study at an appropriate point in time
- communicate the date of the study session to central study organizers to allow for live support
- supervise the session, maybe with the assistance of one or two colleagues
- organize the distribution of the material to participants according to their study group
- communicate the process to participants as necessary
- verify that participants complete the study as intended
- collect all material
- submit all material to the central study organizers

## Timeline

We envision the study taking place in **early to mid-December 2026**. 
At this point, approx. mid-term at KIT, our students have acquired a certain set of skills, but the assignments are still not too complex for the study.
This is our preliminary timeline at KIT. You may adapt it to fit your term schedule and any other constraints.

- Oct. 26: start of lectures
- early to mid-November: announce the study in lecture, via e-mail and in the forum
- mid to late November: finish setup, open registration for students
- Dec. 4: study session
